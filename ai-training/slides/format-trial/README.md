# Format trial — one pair of slides, six ways

Built 2026-08-20 because the Slidev output was judged poor on format and aesthetics.
`DECISIONS.md` records Slidev as adopted **on trial**, against advice that recommended
pandoc to Beamer, with three named reversal triggers. This is the evidence for that decision.

**Rebuild everything:** `./build.sh` from this directory. Outputs land in `out/`.

## What is being compared

Two slides, chosen because between them they exercise everything the course needs:

1. **A figure slide** — full-sentence headline, a figure that has to dominate, a three-label
   evidence row, and a two-source citation footer.
2. **An equation slide** — an equation annotated in place with `\overbrace` and `\underbrace`,
   a fully worked numerical case, and a second figure beside it.

Source is `sample.md` for the five LaTeX variants and `sample-office.md` for the pptx one, which
cannot take raw LaTeX. Both render the real figures out of `assets/figures/`.

## The six outputs

| | File | What it is | The trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | `out/v1-metropolis.pdf` | Beamer + metropolis, unmodified | The modern academic default. Nothing to build or maintain. Dark title band is fixed; the palette is not the course's |
| **V2** | `out/v2-house-light.pdf` | Beamer, custom theme in the house teal | Built to the assertion-evidence spec rather than to a generic theme: sentence headline in a colour band, no navigation chrome, citation rule at the foot. About 20 lines of LaTeX to maintain |
| **V3** | `out/v3-house-dark.pdf` | The same theme inverted | **Shown with the figures unchanged, deliberately.** Every figure is drawn on a white ground, so they sit as bright rectangles on a dark slide. Choosing dark means parameterising the palette in `gen-figures.py` and regenerating every figure in the course |
| **V4** | `out/v4-serif.pdf` | House layout, serif face, rule instead of a band | Reads like a journal page. The serif face is wider, so the body had to drop to `\small` and the footer to `\tiny` to fit — the aesthetic costs you about 15% of the usable area |
| **V5** | `out/v5-editable.pptx` | pandoc straight to pptx | **Text is editable in PowerPoint.** Layout control is close to zero: the two slides came out as four, the headline is centred and oversized, and the label row and citation were pushed onto their own slides. Also open `out/v5-editable.pdf` to see it without opening PowerPoint |
| **V6** | `out/v6-house-light-images.pptx` | V2's PDF, one page per slide as a full-bleed image | The route described as having worked before. Typesetting is preserved exactly; the text is not selectable. Round-trips correctly at 16:9. `pdf2pptx.py` does it in about 30 lines |

## Findings that bear on the toolchain decision

**1. LaTeX typesets the annotated equation better than KaTeX does.** Compare the softmax slide in
any of V1–V4 against `../exports/01-substrate.pdf` page 13. The `\overbrace` and `\underbrace`
annotations, the fraction, and the worked case are all cleaner. Equations were one of the two gaps
`DECISIONS.md` named when Slidev was adopted, and this is the first side-by-side evidence on it.

**2. The Mermaid dependency is smaller than it looks.** Eleven Mermaid blocks exist across all
nineteen decks, and seven of them are in Chapter 1. They are left-to-right flowcharts that TikZ
draws directly, or that could be generated by the matplotlib script that already produces every
other figure in the course. Inline Mermaid was one of the three reasons Slidev was chosen.

**3. Slidev's Mermaid has already cost time.** It renders inside a shadow root, so no stylesheet in
the project can size a diagram, and the `{scale: n}` option sets height only. Three diagrams in
Chapter 1 had to be restructured to stop them overflowing the slide. That finding is recorded in
`../style.css`.

**4. Citations.** The project built a Vue footer component plus a build check that fails on any
source tagged `[U]`. It works, it is done, and it is stricter than what a `.bib` gives by default.
Moving to pandoc would mean either porting that check to the LaTeX path or reverting to
`--citeproc` with a `.bib` and losing the verification gate. **This is the one place where Slidev
is currently ahead**, and it is ahead because of work already paid for.

**5. Both pptx routes give the same answer as Slidev.** Slidev's own PPTX export renders slides as
images, which `DECISIONS.md` records as an accepted loss. V6 does exactly the same thing from a
PDF. V5 is the only route that yields editable text, and it costs the layout.

**6. Nothing here needs a paid account.** Canva, Gamma and the rest would mean the deck stops being
text under version control, which is the premise Chapter 15 teaches. Every variant above is built
from a markdown file by a script in this repository.

## Recommendation

**Move to pandoc + Beamer, using V2 or V4, and do it now rather than later.**

The reasons, in order of weight:

- Eighteen of nineteen decks are being rewritten anyway. The migration cost is close to zero
  today and rises with every chapter rebuilt in Slidev.
- Equations are visibly better, and the course carries a roofline derivation in Chapter 7 that is
  harder than anything tried here.
- The deck becomes the Chapter 15 demonstration of its own claim, which was in the original advice.
- The Mermaid dependency, the main reason to stay, turns out to be eleven diagrams.

**The alternative I rejected: keep Slidev and write a custom theme.** It would reach V2's
appearance — the aesthetics complaint is about the default theme, not about Slidev — and it
preserves the citation check that already works. It loses because it does not fix the equations,
does not remove the shadow-root problem, and spends theme-authoring effort on a toolchain whose
two remaining advantages are one deck's worth of diagrams and a component that would have to be
rebuilt anyway if the decision is ever revisited.

**What moving costs, stated honestly:** porting the `[U]`-tag citation check to the pandoc path,
converting eleven Mermaid diagrams, and re-authoring Chapter 1's 21 slides in pandoc markdown.
Roughly a day, most of it the citation check.

## What was not tested

- **No rehearsal on a projector.** Contrast and font size are judged on screen only.
- **V3 was not given dark-adapted figures**, on purpose, so the cost of choosing dark is visible.
- **`--citeproc` with a real `.bib` was not wired up.** Finding 4 describes the mechanism; it was
  not built, so the claim that it would lose the `[U]` gate is an argument rather than a measurement.
- **PowerPoint itself was not used.** V5 and V6 were checked by converting back to PDF through
  LibreOffice. Fonts and spacing may differ in PowerPoint proper.
- **No accessibility check** — contrast ratios, colour-blind safety, screen-reader order. That gap
  is also open in `research-slide-design.md`.
