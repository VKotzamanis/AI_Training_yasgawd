# AI Training for a Civil & Environmental Engineering Research Group

Teaching materials for a three-session AI training delivered to PhD researchers.

## Audience

PhD researchers in civil and environmental engineering. MATLAB is common; terminal experience is rare. Access on the day: individual Claude Pro or Max subscriptions, a group-owned local inference machine running Qwen and image generation, and Gemini via Antigravity.

## Sessions

| Session | Theme | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How these things work and where they fail | 1–8 |
| 2 | Tools | 9–13 |
| 3 | Research practice | 14–19 |

Roughly four hours each, run weekly. The gap between sessions is deliberate: attendees return having used the tools, with real questions instead of hypothetical ones.

## What makes this course different from the free alternatives

Anthropic Academy already teaches Claude Code, MCP and the API for free. Three things here don't exist elsewhere:

1. **The training pipeline taught from inside it.** The instructor worked as a human-feedback annotator. Chapter 2 traces observed model behaviour back to specific pressures in the rating process.
2. **Domain specificity.** Every exercise runs on the attendee's own MATLAB code, their own data, their own literature.
3. **Method over folklore.** Chapter 4 gives attendees a way to test prompting claims on their own model and their own task, rather than a list of tips.
